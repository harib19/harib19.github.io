Why Dr. RTL's axil_cdc result is unsafe

The promoted rewrite duplicates a synchronizer stage. EQY and simulation pass, because they cannot model metastability. Simulating 2,000 random AXI-Lite writes with metastability injection over 10 seeds: the original corrupts 0 words, while Dr. RTL's rewrite corrupts 1,430–1,583 of 4,000 address and data words (≈38%).

Candidate audit and the vanilla baseline

Of the 60 candidates Dr. RTL accepted as correct, 9 (15%) are CDC-unsafe, all on axil_cdc. A vanilla LLM loop with no guards shows big timing gains (+87% mean WNS), but 0 of 3 of its multi-clock finals are equivalent to the original. That is why the formal gate matters.

Ablation: same loop, guardrails off

On soc, afifo, async_fifo and axil_cdc. With the locks and the CDC re-check only measured, not enforced, the final axil_cdc design rewrites the locked capture logic and fails the CDC re-check, the same hazard Dr. RTL produces. With the guardrails on, every Aegis final is CDC-clean.

On multi-clock designs Aegis's timing is competitive, not better. Mean WNS gain is about equal, and Dr. RTL has the larger TNS gain on async_fifo, prim_fifo_async and drrtl_fifo_2clk, where Aegis traded total slack for worst-domain slack. Aegis used fewer tokens on 6 of 7 paired designs (median ≈2×).

CDC mutation benchmark
